Everything JukaHub does

One controller-first app on top of the stock firmware, for watching, playing alone or together, streaming, keeping your saves safe and reaching your other devices. Here it is, one area at a time. Items marked (Preview) are new and still being confirmed on every handheld.

Home

The screen you start on, in the shape you like, with everything a press or two away.

  • The Tabs HomeThe default: a strip of tabs in the top bar (Recent, Main, Games, Entertainment, Favourites and Collections, and a Tools tab if you want one) that L1 and R1 walk through. Each tab, folder and collection keeps its own view: grid, rows, list, carousel or compact, and L2 reorders and hides tabs. A Backdrop view fills the screen with the highlighted game's screenshot, a game can be pinned to Main, and collections fill themselves: Last played, Most played, Recently added, Never played, and one per genre if you want them. (Preview) Tabs Home
  • Five more layoutsStandard (the dashboard with Continue and Media), a hero grid, a carousel of big cards, widgets you arrange, and console-style homes such as PS5 Style, Switch 2 Style and Xbox One Style. Minimal UI turns Home into a plain list of names.
  • Edit HomeL2 edits Home in place: move and resize tiles on square cells, arrange the top bar, and pin Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and Power tiles that switch the radio with one press.
  • Search everythingY searches apps, games, settings, files and the web. A settings result opens the exact row, and you can type, say it or send it from your phone.
  • Quick Settings and the quick menuSelect on Home, or Start → Device from anywhere: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, volume, brightness, stick lights, Quiet Mode, the magnifier, Record screen and Close all apps, laid out as tiles, a compact grid or a list, and edited with L2. The quick menu puts big Recents, Collections, Games and Tools tiles over Wi-Fi, Sleep, Restart and Power off, and can be what Menu opens on Home. (Preview)
  • Continue and the daily challengeEvery launch is recorded, so Continue resumes the last video, podcast or game in one press, with a picture of where you left the game. Widgets show your most played game and this month's play time. Once a day Home can offer a game from your library with a goal, for a trophy of its own. (Preview)
  • The manual on the deviceSettings → About opens the controls and a quick start that walks a new player through Home, games and Settings, offline, and Home shows one tip a day. On a keyboard, F1 lists the keys for the screen you are on. Controls

Watch and listen

Everything you watch and listen to, with the D-pad you already have.

  • YouTubeSearch, Continue watching and Trending, with Shorts, Downloads, History, Favorites, Library, Live TV and Podcasts as tabs on the same screen. Line results up in a queue, and B puts a video aside without losing your place. A video that will not stream downloads and plays from the card instead, with its progress on screen.
  • Downloads for offlineSave a video, or just its sound, from the player or from Start → Tools → Downloads: a queue with live progress, pause and resume and per-row retry, with subtitles kept beside the video. Long downloads can carry on in the background while you do something else.
  • Live TV and podcastsChannels from your own .m3u and .m3u8 playlists or the public iptv-org list, grouped by category, and a podcast library with subscriptions and new episodes that survives an offline start.
  • Music and radioOpen any audio file and the player takes its folder: shuffle that visits each track once, repeat, a play-next queue, saved playlists that go in and out as M3U8, favourites, 0.75× to 2× speed, a sleep timer, synced .lrc lyrics, a full-screen spectrum and a listening history. Audiobooks show their chapters and each keeps its own speed. Lyrics stored inside a song show too, and a Radio shelf opens the free Radio Browser directory of stations. (Preview) Playback continues while you browse. Music player
  • Game soundtracksThe music player plays the music inside game files: NES (NSF), SNES (SPC), Game Boy (GBS), Sega (VGM), PC Engine, MSX and Commodore 64 (SID) tunes, and MOD, XM, S3M and IT modules. A soundtrack opens as an album with a row per song and the game's cover from your library, and plays on with the screen off. (Preview) Game soundtracks
  • Background musicOff by default: Calm, Lo-fi or Chiptune, written as Synth songs and played live, a song of your own from the Synth, or your music library on shuffle. It fades out whenever anything else wants the speaker.
  • Video playerYour own videos from the card with subtitles, A/V sync that keeps the picture on the sound, video filters, and notifications during a video only if you want them. The settings sheet picks a film's audio track and its subtitles, from .srt, .ass, .ssa and MicroDVD .sub files or the film's own text tracks, and remembers both per file. (Preview)
  • Books and photosAn EPUB and PDF reader that remembers where you left off, and an image viewer for PNG, JPG, BMP and WebP: Left and Right browse the folder, L1 and R1 zoom, and X rotates, renames or deletes. With KOReader on the card, comics, MOBI, FB2 and DjVu books open in it from Files. (Preview)
  • BrowserA web browser built for the D-pad that runs page scripts against a real document, fills in forms, zooms text, blocks ads and trackers (and says how many), remembers where you were reading, and plays YouTube links in Media. Open on your phone shows the page's address as a QR code, to carry on reading there. (Preview) Browser

Play

Your ROM library as a library, and the things nobody expects a handheld launcher to do.

  • Games libraryEvery console on the card with its covers, game counts and play time, as a list, a 3D shelf or a cover column, with search, letter scrubbing, a random pick, hidden games and video snaps. It reads the stock firmware's, CrossMix's and NextUI's folders where they are. Cover Flow and a cube that turns to the next console are new views, and the library now opens at once from an index of the card, with games copied over USB, FTP or from your phone appearing by themselves. (Preview) Games library
  • PlayStation 2, GameCube and morePlayStation 2 games play in the ARMSX2 core on every TrimUI model: the first start downloads the core and the files it needs and copies your PS2 BIOS into place. GameCube and Wii play in Dolphin on the Smart Pro S (the other models can try anyway), and original Xbox games in hakuX on the Smart Pro, which installs itself. NAOMI, Atomiswave, Nintendo DSi, OpenBOR, J2ME phone games and Godot games have shelves of their own, and a ScummVM adventure or a DOS game kept as a folder is one game, with controls ready. (Preview) PlayStation 2 · GameCube and Wii
  • Covers that find themselvesBox art is matched by itself, including for names that do not match the reference title, and games the usual indexes lack get a box from the LaunchBox Games Database. (Preview) A homebrew, hack or translation with no cover anywhere gets one from two minutes of its own gameplay, and you can choose or remove a cover by hand.
  • The right emulator for each gameWhen a console has several emulators, Which emulator? lists them, the one recommended for your handheld first, and remembers your pick. Get more cores downloads the RetroArch cores worth having for the consoles on your card, and a new core build stays on probation until a game runs on it.
  • Game ReadyBefore a game starts, JukaHub checks what it needs: the game file and its discs, the emulator and core, and the BIOS in the folder RetroArch really reads. Instead of a black screen, a game that cannot start says which file is missing and where it goes, and A fixes what it can. (Preview) Game Ready
  • A page for every gameA game's page has a More button for what belongs to that title: its manual and guides, a control layout (arcade six-button, Neo Geo, DS stylus), Game Lab, which compares two setups you play, by the frames each really showed, its saves on another JukaHub, and, for Game Boy games that print, a printer scrapbook. (Preview) More on a game's page
  • The in-game menuHold Select + L2 in any game JukaHub started: six save slots with pictures in RetroArch games, quick settings for brightness, volume, Wi-Fi and the lights, the music you are listening to, the game's manual, Ask Juka, sleep, and Quit to JukaHub with the save written. In a RetroArch game, Menu + Select saves and quits in one press. (Preview) Holding Select + Start for three seconds ends a game that is stuck.
  • Pick up and playRetroArch games resume from their automatic save state and sleep lasts. The game switcher shows your recent games side by side, each with a picture of where you left it, and the handheld can power on straight into the last game. On NextUI, JukaHub works in place, with your ROM and BIOS folders where they already are, and cheats work for NextUI games too. (Preview)
  • Emulation settingsPer console or for all of them: looks (scanlines, LCD grid, CRT and Pixel transparency), overlays of your own, overscan, aspect ratio, widescreen on the 16:9 models for 3D games that have a hack, fast-forward speed, audio quality, latency and run-ahead, a CPU limit per game, and a shader speed check that says whether a look keeps full speed. RetroArch's hotkeys can be set per console and per game in Settings → Controls. (Preview) Looks
  • RetroAchievements and trophiesRetroAchievements progress per game, in RetroArch and in JukaHub's own PPSSPP. RetroArch's achievement options (hardcore, leaderboards, encore) can be set for JukaHub's launches, and in casual mode unlocks keep counting with no internet and are sent when you are back online. (Preview) 391 of JukaHub's own trophies reward what you play, watch and make, and optional daily limits and break reminders are there for anyone who wants them.
  • Light gunGun games in RetroArch play with the pad as the gun: the right stick aims (the D-pad on a Brick), R1 fires and L1 reloads, with a practice target to tune it per game. About a hundred known gun games, from Duck Hunt to Time Crisis, switch it on by themselves. (Preview) Light gun
  • PortMaster and the Ports StorePortMaster's whole catalogue as a store, filtered for the handheld you hold. JukaHub installs PortMaster and keeps it current, and installs and updates ports itself, each checked against the catalogue, with no Python and without opening PortMaster's menu. Installed ports play from the library with Continue, play time and the in-game menu. (Preview) PortMaster
  • Emulators that stay currentEvery core in the ARM64 buildbot index is one press away, a daily pass updates what JukaHub installed and keeps the old build for a one-press roll back, and RetroArch can start on JukaHub's own newer C library, so the newest cores run on the stock firmware too. (Preview) JukaHub builds its own current PPSSPP for the Smart Pro and Brick. Per-game compatibility reports and boost plans cover N64, Dreamcast and PSP. Compatibility
  • PICO-8, homebrew and JukaGamesIf you own PICO-8, your carts start in the official PICO-8 with Splore. Get Homebrew now also brings TIC-80 carts and RPG Maker 2000 and 2003 games, CHIP-8 games play inside JukaHub itself, and WASM-4 carts have a shelf that sets up its core on the first start. (Preview) HTML5 games play in JukaHub's own game browser, and JukaGames are built in: JukaLand, the Synth, Solitaire and minigames from Snake to 2048. Homebrew
  • Screenshots, clips and time-lapsesHold Menu for a screenshot anywhere, or press Select + R1 in a game; the screen flashes and shows what it caught. Record screen films whatever is on the panel, games included, as a silent MP4. (Preview) Recordings lists the clips, and a game can leave a short time-lapse film of the session. Recordings
  • Manage the games themselvesRename a game's file together with everything named after it, delete a game and its files after a second press, hide it, or hand it to its tool. Disc helpers write the cue sheets and multi-disc playlists PlayStation and Sega CD rips need, and the ROM manager finds duplicates and cut-off zips.
  • For the tinkerersA memory scanner that finds and changes values in a running game, a controller test for drifting sticks, and game diagnosis for "why won't it start?". Memory scanner

Play together

Games for more than one person, in the same room or not, and a handheld that more than one person can share.

  • NetplayX on a game hosts it or joins a room another JukaHub is hosting on your Wi-Fi, with your own copy of the ROM. Host online through libretro's public relays to play a friend elsewhere, with no port forwarding. Nintendo 64, Dreamcast, arcade and multitap games now take up to four players. (Preview)
  • A link cable over Wi-FiTwo handhelds trade and battle with their own Game Boy games, Pokémon Red with Blue, through a link cable that runs over Wi-Fi, and the GBA's wireless adapter opens the Union Room in FireRed, LeafGreen and Emerald. With no Wi-Fi around, the hotspot connects them. (Preview)
  • Party Mode: Guess the GameTools → Party Mode makes the handheld a game-show host. Up to 12 players scan its QR code and play from their phone's browser, with no app, account or internet: a cover from your library zooms out step by step, the first to buzz picks among four titles, and the handheld keeps the score. With the hotspot on, the party needs no router. (Preview) Party Mode
  • Watch PartyHost on one handheld and join from the others on the same Wi-Fi with a five-character code. Anyone in the room can queue a link, or play, pause and seek for everyone.
  • Chat and voiceThe community Discord channel on the handheld: read, reply, share links and files, and record voice clips. Chat's Voice tab joins the community voice room in one press, with no account to make, and a phone or PC joins from a QR code with any Mumble app. (Preview) An unread badge sits on Home. Chat voice
  • Controller ModeTools → Controller Mode turns the handheld into a game controller: over a USB cable as an Xbox 360 controller for Windows and Steam, over Wi-Fi to a small receiver the handheld serves itself, as a DSU source for PC emulators such as Dolphin and Cemu, or as a Bluetooth gamepad (beta). A tap on Menu brings you back to JukaHub. (Preview) Controller Mode
  • Two handhelds, one libraryTwo paired handhelds share saves, save states, BIOS files, collections, recents and play time, and, only if you switch it on, the ROMs one of them lacks, copying only what is missing. A game's page shows its saves here and on the other JukaHub side by side and moves them with one press. (Preview) Device Sync
  • Profiles and GuestEveryone who plays the handheld can keep their own RetroArch saves, Continue list, play time, trophies and look; switch from the top bar, or have it ask who is playing at start. Guest plays without touching anyone's progress, and its RetroArch saves are wiped when the session ends. Standalone emulators such as PPSSPP keep one set of saves for everyone and say so first. (Preview) Profiles
  • Kid and Kiosk modeKiosk mode is for playing only: games, favourites, music and video work, while stores, installs, Files, the Terminal and updates are locked. Kid mode also shows only the games marked for kids and locks the internet apps. A parent code, or the Konami code if you set none, leaves either one. (Preview) Kid mode

Stream

Games that run on another machine, played on the one in your hands. The PS4, PS5, X1 and PC pills on Home open each in one press.

  • PS4 and PS5 Remote PlayJukaHub's own client: it scans the LAN, pairs with the console's Link Device PIN, wakes the console, and streams through its native chiaki-stream helper with every DualSense input mapped and an on-screen display. A PS5 also needs your PSN account ID once, which Sign in with PlayStation or your phone can fill in. A PS4 or PS5 pad works over Bluetooth where the firmware supports it, and over USB always, and a stream reconnects after the handheld sleeps. Remote Play
  • X1: your XboxStreams an Xbox One or Series console through GreenOvercast, a native client for ARM handhelds that JukaHub installs through PortMaster. You sign in once with a code on your phone. It needs a newer C library than stock TrimUI firmware has, so JukaHub keeps a private copy on the SD card for it and leaves the system's own alone. Xbox boost gives a stream or an Xbox game the whole handheld while it runs: every core, the GPU at full speed where the firmware allows, and JukaHub's memory handed back. (Preview) Private C runtime
  • PC streamingJukaHub's own Moonlight client for Vibepollo, Apollo, Sunshine and GeForce Experience. PCs show up by themselves or by address, pairing shows a PIN and a QR of the PC's PIN page, and your PC's library appears with its covers. It asks for exactly the handheld's screen size, and Check this handheld measures what the device can decode and picks the quality. (Preview) PC streaming
  • Cloud GamingXbox Cloud Gaming (Game Pass) and GeForce NOW, from Start → Games, streamed through GreenOvercast. The page says plainly what Game Pass can and cannot do on an ARM handheld. (Preview) Cloud Gaming
  • SteamApps → Steam lists every game on your account with its cover, play time and size, signed in by scanning a code with the Steam app (no password is typed). A Handheld tab finds the games PortMaster has a port of and can copy their files from a paired JukaHub PC. (Preview)

Juka, the assistant

Say what you want instead of finding it. Voice command

  • The voice orbPress Menu (F4 on a keyboard) and a glowing orb listens. Say "open YouTube", "volume 50", a game, a song or a settings page, or change a setting by voice; teach it your own words. A question that names nothing on the handheld goes to Juka. Type instead sits under the orb while it waits.
  • Offline on the Smart ProThe original Smart Pro recognises speech on the device, in English with a fast local model and in other languages with Whisper; the package downloads by itself once. When the model needs room, JukaHub frees memory by itself, and an offline voice can speak the answers (Preview); a free Groq key, pasted from your phone, makes it all faster. Offline AI
  • "Hey Juka"An optional wake word on the Smart Pro opens the orb hands-free, or the game assistant during a game. "Hey Juka, open YouTube" works in one breath.
  • The game assistantHold Select + R2 in a game on a TrimUI handheld: it pauses and Juka listens. Ask about the game in front of you and the answer is shown and read aloud. (Preview)
  • Translate the game screenTurn on Translate games, and in a RetroArch game the in-game menu's Translate screen sends a picture of the game, with your Groq key, for the text in your language, shown over the game like subtitles or read aloud in English. A translation service of your own works too, and nothing is stored. (Preview)
  • Juka can seeWith a Groq key, ask "what's this?" or "read this" and a picture of the screen goes with the question; it is never saved. Juka can also look at pictures and videos you choose.
  • Juka AI with device toolsIn Chat, Juka can check the device before it answers "why won't my last game start?", "free up space" or "make games run smoother": its logs, storage, settings and network. Looking is free; every change waits for your OK on screen. (Preview) Device tools
  • Lights that listenOn the Smart Pro the stick lights follow your voice while the orb listens, and glow while Juka thinks.

Keep

Progress lives in folders on a removable card. JukaHub treats that seriously.

  • Every save checkedEach save of a library game is read back after the emulator writes it, and the game's page shows the last good one: "Saved 4 min ago", or, honestly, "Not saved since it closed unexpectedly". A low battery wakes a sleeping game to save it and power off, standalone emulators such as PPSSPP and DraStic are closed cleanly before the power goes, and the card is made read-only just before the power goes. (Preview) Session safety
  • Save Manager and Save guardSnapshots of every emulator save folder (RetroArch, PPSSPP, DraStic, Flycast), restored only after a safety snapshot of what is there now. Save guard takes one quietly at every start when a save really changed and keeps the last five. Snapshots are plain files with a checksum each, so they work without JukaHub. Save Manager
  • Save syncA game's saves come down from your cloud before it starts and go up after it ends: Google Drive, signed in from your phone, or WebDAV (Nextcloud, ownCloud, Koofr or a NAS). Syncthing can share the saves and, if you want, your screenshots, music, books and ROMs, and a phone resume bundle is there too. (Preview) Save sync
  • RomM serverConnect to your own RomM library: browse and download games with their covers, fetch BIOS files, and let saves sync by themselves. The address and token can come from your phone. (Preview) RomM
  • RecoverySafe mode on L + R + Start, a recovery card with the previous version and your last good settings, a crash report offered at the next start, and a low-battery save and suspend. An update that hangs, crashes or stops answering the buttons in its first starts is put back by itself within two minutes, with a card that says what happened. (Preview) Recovery
  • Tested on this deviceJukaHub notes, for your model, what it saw work for each game, port and app: it starts, the controls answer, there is sound, it survives a sleep and it quits back cleanly. A game's page says "Verified on Smart Pro" or names the known issue, and Device check walks through the buttons, sound, sleep, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi and saves a report. (Preview) Pick up and play
  • Move to a new SD cardSettings → About copies everything, from games, saves and BIOS files to ports, artwork and JukaHub itself, onto a new card in a USB reader. Every file is read back from the new card and compared with the old one, a pulled reader or a flat battery resumes where it stopped, and JukaHub says when the old card can be reused. (Preview) Moving to a new card
  • Card and battery healthCard check reads the card's own health flags and every file, lists the ones it can no longer read and can test for a fake card (Preview); battery health and time left; and Library doctor, which finds the same game copied twice and zips cut off mid-copy.
  • Settings that stayYour settings live in jukauser.json and survive every update; Power tools keeps snapshots of it with a diff and rollback. A factory reset copies everything it erases into one undo file first. (Preview)
  • Curation HubAn essentials checklist for a new card, shareable theme and text-size profiles tuned per panel, and compatibility votes for games and PortMaster titles. It works offline. Curation Hub

Connect

The handheld, your phone, your PC, a TV and the rest of the house.

  • Wi-FiA real network picker: scan, join with the on-screen keyboard, reconnect saved networks without retyping the password, disconnect and forget, and a diagnostics sheet you can copy when something is wrong. Wi-Fi
  • Bluetooth that behaves like a phone'sPair pads, headphones and keyboards in JukaHub. Remembered devices reconnect by themselves, the sound follows a headset and pauses when it drops, each output keeps its own volume, and a pad shows its battery. (Preview)
  • Phone companionScan the QR on Home and your phone shows the handheld's screen, types into the focused field, works as a remote, sends links, and drops games, covers, music and BIOS files straight into the right folders. It also browses the card's shared folders, and sends a Groq key, a PSN account ID or a RomM token without typing on the handheld. Whole folders now go in one drop, and the page can add tasks and start a JukaHub update. (Preview) How it works
  • Screen ShareThe Smart Pro has no HDMI port, so JukaHub sends its screen, games included, to a browser on a TV, laptop or phone on your Wi-Fi, for up to six viewers. It is off until you turn it on, and the pairing code is painted over in the picture.
  • VNC and TelnetAny VNC app on your network can see and drive the handheld, games included, with a keyboard's keys acting as the pad; Telnet gives a computer the Terminal's shell. Both are off until you start them in Tools → Network tools → Servers & sharing, ask for a password, answer only on your own network, and are not encrypted. (Preview) VNC and Telnet
  • FTP and SFTPDrop games, BIOS files and media from a PC over Wi-Fi: SFTP is encrypted, both use a password made for each start, and both switch themselves off when nobody uses them. Copy files from a PC
  • The card as a network driveThe SMB share puts the SD card in Windows Explorer and the Mac's Finder as a drive, through JukaHub's own SMB server, so no Samba is needed; the WebDAV share offers the game and media folders to any WebDAV client. Both stay off until you start them and stop after 15 idle minutes. (Preview) SMB share
  • USB file transfer and OTGOver a cable, the SD card shows up on your computer as a media device (MTP), with no app to install, or, in USB storage mode on the Smart Pro, Brick and Hammer with stock firmware, as a plain USB drive. Tools → USB & OTG names what is plugged into the USB-C port, switches it to host mode, mounts a USB stick, copies games off it and ejects it safely. (Preview) USB file transfer · USB and OTG
  • Games on a network driveSettings → Network → Network drives adds a NAS or PC share over SMB, WebDAV or SFTP. Its games appear on their consoles' shelves with a network badge, Download and play copies one to the card, and the copy can be removed again while its saves stay. (Preview) Network drives
  • HotspotThe handheld becomes a Wi-Fi network of its own, so two handhelds can play netplay and a phone can reach the Companion with no router. (Preview) Hotspot
  • Tailscale built inTools → Network tools → Tailscale installs Tailscale's official build, checked against its published SHA-256, and signs in with a QR code. Then SSH, FTP, the phone companion, VNC and the SMB share answer on its tailnet address from anywhere. It runs only while switched on, on Wi-Fi and above 15% battery. (Preview) Tailscale
  • VPN and DNSSend the whole device through a WireGuard tunnel from your provider or your own server, importing the .conf from the card, Files or your phone. Settings → Network → DNS picks Cloudflare, Google, Quad9, AdGuard, family filtering or your own servers, and can find the fastest. VPN · DNS
  • Mail and calendarA Mail app with an unread count on its tile, your calendars in the clock's month view and on a widget, and reminders before events. Accounts are added from your phone, and passwords and tokens are stored encrypted. (Preview) Mail and calendar
  • Smart homeDrive Home Assistant entities, mirror every toggle to MQTT, and see Pi-hole and your router from the couch. Smart home

Make it yours

Every screen is data, so how it looks is up to you.

  • Over 50 themesFrom Dark, Light and OLED to Nord, Dracula, Synthwave, Matrix and Fallout, complete Home conversions, console styles, and community themes that install and update over the air. Theme files are plain JSON you can write yourself. Theme files
  • Colours, packs and console namesEvery colour row opens a picker with a live swatch and a contrast check. Background, text and focus-ring colours sit on top of any theme, eight palettes set them all at once, and glass can let the wallpaper through. Look packs switch console icons, the menu colour and the startup sound in one press and get a boot logo ready to install, and console names can follow your region: Genesis or Mega Drive, TurboGrafx-16 or PC Engine. (Preview) Look packs
  • Glass, water and wallpapersClassic or realistic glass, a liquid background that ripples where the focus lands and stirs with the sticks (R2 sets how much), animated GIF wallpapers, and a focus ring that bounces between buttons.
  • Seven languagesEnglish, Español, Português (Brasil), Français, Deutsch, Italiano and Русский, chosen at the first start and changed at any time, with dates and numbers that follow your language.
  • AccessibilityText from 85% to 145%, a legible reading font, high contrast, reduce transparency and three colour-blind-safe palettes; switch access, a magnifier that follows the focus, adjustable timing and sticky chords; mono or one-ear audio, balance and captions; a system-wide button remap and a one-handed chord. Narration reads the focused button, its value and what A will do, with the offline voice, and holding L1 + R1 for three seconds turns it on from anywhere. (Preview) Accessibility
  • Dock and ambientWhile it charges or sits idle: a large clock with the next alarm, today's weather, what is playing, new chat messages and a slideshow from your Photos folder or your games' pictures. With no music on the card, Play tunes in to internet radio. (Preview) Y arms a sleep timer and X blanks the screen while audio keeps playing. Dock and ambient
  • It knows your nameTell it once and the boot and the shutdown greet you by name, out loud if you like (Preview), with a startup chime if you want one.
  • The web builderLay out your own screens in the browser, push them to the handheld over Wi-Fi with Live Builder, export jukaconfig.json, or share a design as a single link. Open the builder

Your handheld

The parts of a handheld's operating system that the stock menu leaves out.

  • JukaHub OSSettings → JukaHub OS → Boot into JukaHub OS and the handheld powers on straight into JukaHub, which then runs every game and app inside its own session. Nothing on the firmware's partitions is written, and turning the switch off makes it an app again. JukaHub OS
  • A fast startBy default Home appears as soon as it is ready, straight after the firmware's logo, and a start remembers what it needed last time so the next one reads less. Boot animations (Cinematic, Roll, Still logo, Classic) are a setting away. Games start sooner too: a game left highlighted is read into memory at the lowest priority, so A waits less on the card. (Preview) Boot
  • Automatic CPU speedOn by default on the handheld: the CPU slows down on Home and in menus and speeds up for games, videos and the Synth, and remembers what each game needed. Heavy consoles can have the GPU at full speed while they play, and handhelds with 1 GB of memory get extra room from swap. (Preview) Close all apps and games frees memory in one press and says how much came back. Device tuning
  • Display and stick lightsBrightness, the lock-screen timeout, contrast, saturation and the rest of the screen in one Settings page, with fonts you can download. The Smart Pro's stick lights have their own page: colour, effect, brightness and a smooth mode, the battery level on L2 + R2 on Home, and a rainbow when an achievement unlocks. (Preview)
  • TV modePlug a Brick Pro into a TV over USB-C and JukaHub switches to TV mode by itself: larger text, a USB or Bluetooth controller as player 1 in the RetroArch games it starts, and the built-in screen dark if you want. Settings → Display → TV mode turns it on for any device. (Preview) TV mode
  • Locks and the FN switchA startup lock with a button code, a password or your voice, stored only as a salted hash. On the Smart Pro the FN switch can be Travel lock, which keeps the handheld asleep in a bag, or Mute, Lights off or Airplane mode, and sliding it back puts back exactly what it changed. (Preview)
  • Power and sleepSleep on the power button and in the in-game menu, power off after a sleep of 15 minutes to 48 hours (Preview), battery time left in Quick Settings, a guided battery calibration, and Quiet Mode that holds toasts and sounds but never an alarm. Battery warnings are yours to set, the charge can show over a game as it drops, and a game left idle can dim the screen and then sleep. (Preview)
  • Clock, alarms and dateThe clock sets itself from the network, a time-zone picker covers the rest, a month calendar opens from the top bar, and a ringing alarm takes over the screen until you answer it.
  • Spatial audioVirtual surround on headphones and wider stereo on the speakers, for JukaHub's videos and music and the sound of every game and app it starts. Off until you turn it on. Spatial audio
  • Controls your wayStick calibration and dead zones, a virtual cursor on the right stick, mouse mode on Select + L1, and vibration strength from soft to strong. Pick a Nintendo or Xbox button layout, choose what the Brick's F1 and F2 keys and the Smart Pro S's Home key do, and let the left stick be the D-pad for consoles that never had a stick. (Preview) Controls

Security

Tools → Security & trust shows all of this live on the device, without a PC.

  • Checked updates, signed packagesEvery update is checked against the SHA-256 digest GitHub publishes before it is unpacked, and against an Ed25519 signature when the release carries one. Patch installs only from an Ed25519-signed repository index, with each archive checked against it. HTTPS alone is never treated as proof.
  • Updates on your termsStay on Stable for tested releases or switch to Beta, install now or at the next start, and never over a running game. (Preview) An interrupted update rolls back by itself.
  • Encrypted secretsAPI keys, tokens and mail and network-drive passwords are AES-256-GCM with a fresh nonce per value, and the logs hide the secrets kept in your settings. Set your own key with JUKAHUB_CRYPTO_KEY: the built-in default is the same on every install, and Security & trust says when it is still in use.
  • Downloads from an allowlistHelper tools resolve only from repositories listed in the code, through a size-capped downloader, and run directly, never through a shell. Tailscale and Java are checked against the SHA-256 their makers publish, and the PlayStation 2 core against GitHub's checksum when it lists one. (Preview)
  • Servers you can seeFTP, SFTP, the SMB and WebDAV shares, VNC and Telnet start only when you press their row, Screen Share and Controller Mode's links only when you switch them on, and Party Mode only while its screen is open. VNC, Telnet and the SMB share answer only on your home network and your tailnet. (Preview) The posture view names whatever is listening.
  • No telemetry unless you askAnonymous diagnostics are off by default and limited to build and device facts. Privacy
